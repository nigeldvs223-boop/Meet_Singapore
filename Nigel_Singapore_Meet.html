2. Objectives for the Singapore trip

  1. Test the demand story. Hear from people close to Singapore’s SAF mandate how feedstock is actually bought and what “certified” has to mean.
  2. Meet the real UCO and SAF operators. Use Pratik Shah and Plug and Play’s summit to reach feedstock aggregators, refiners and airline sustainability teams.
  3. Learn how operators scaled across countries (Zachary Wang, Harry Yu, Sejin Shin) and how impact capital thinks about early climate ventures (Mahesh Joshi).
  4. Leave with named contacts and permission to follow up, not pitches.

3. Context that shapes every conversation

  • Singapore requires SAF use on departing flights from 2026, with a 1% target rising to 3–5% by 2030, funded by a ticket levy [S3]. The levy applies to tickets sold from 1 April 2026 for flights departing from 1 October 2026 [S2].
  • Singapore is a feedstock buyer and processor hub. Apeiron Bioenergy, a Singapore biodiesel producer, reports 10 countries of operation and over 600 million litres of UCO supplied since 2017, and has financed UCO collection points and pre treatment [S4]. Neste has agreed to supply 1,000 t of neat SAF from its Singapore refinery to Singapore Airlines and Scoot (same source).
  • People to look for at the summit and SWITCH (not on the schedule, not verified as attending): Apeiron Bioenergy, Neste Singapore, SIA and Scoot sustainability teams, CAAS.

5. Business assumptions DashBin still has to validate

  • Hotel UCO volumes in Kochi are large and concentrated enough to justify scheduled pickup.
  • Quality (FFA, moisture, oil blend) is good enough for a share of oil to qualify for SAF grade pathways.
  • Hotels will change collector for price, fixed pickup or documentation.
  • Buyers will pay a premium for traceable, audit ready batches.
  • Collection logistics can be cheap enough at small ticket volumes.

6. Introductions worth pursuing

  1. A UCO aggregator or processor operator (via Pratik Shah or the summit).
  2. An airline or airport sustainability contact who handles SAF procurement.
  3. A certification or traceability practitioner (ISCC type auditing).
  4. An impact investor who has backed a waste or circular economy business in Asia (via Mahesh Joshi).
  5. An operations leader who built multi city logistics (Harry Yu).

7. Standards for every conversation

  • Open with something they actually did, then ask one question.
  • One small ask each: a name, a resource, or permission to follow up.
  • Do not mention investment, partnership or an introduction until the conversation has earned it.
  • Closing line: “I’m not asking for a commitment. If you give me one standard to be judged against, I’ll come back with data.”
